# LTA Tax Academy

Case study: redesigning an e-learning platform around the student journey.

- **Type:** Product Design
- **Industry:** E-learning, SaaS
- **Role:** Product Designer
- **Year:** 2024
- **Duration:** 2 months
- **Users:** Latin American accountants living and working in the U.S.

## What is LTA Tax Academy?

LTA Tax Academy provides professional tax courses and certifications for Latin American accountants living and working in the United States.

## What was the context?

The existing platform was built with WordPress. It combined the marketing experience with the student classroom. As the platform grew, this created friction for both students and the internal team.

- **Marketing side:** landing pages, course catalog, blog and SEO content, sales calls to action, promotions.
- **Learning side:** student dashboard, my courses, course player, certificates, progress tracking.

Both lived in the same platform and competed for attention. The learning side was buried under marketing, and students couldn't find what they needed. The platform wasn't built around their goals.

## What was the problem?

Students weren't struggling with learning. They were struggling with the platform. Support tickets, course surveys, and stakeholder feedback showed three recurring problems across the student journey:

1. **Find my course.** Students struggled to find courses they had purchased or discover new ones.
2. **Continue learning.** There was no clear path to resume a course they had already started.
3. **Use the platform.** Slow performance, responsive issues, and WordPress limitations created more friction.

Supporting evidence:

- 5–7 support tickets per day, recurring and platform-related.
- Average course satisfaction of 2.5/5, pointing to broader dissatisfaction with the experience.
- Typical membership of about 1 month before students churned from the platform.

## What research did you do?

The process started with understanding the existing product instead of creating new screens right away.

- **Existing platform evaluation:** usability, navigation, responsive, performance, and content-structure problems.
- **Customer support tickets:** recurring problems around finding courses, continuing courses, and platform performance.
- **Course surveys:** feedback averaged about 2.5/5.
- **Stakeholder conversations:** worked with the business and technical teams to understand student problems and internal constraints, including difficulties managing courses in WordPress.
- **Technical team discussions:** early engineering collaboration surfaced platform constraints that shaped design decisions.
- **Competitive research:** reviewed established e-learning platforms to see how they structure learning environments, course discovery, progress, and retention.

## What was the key insight?

The platform was trying to serve two different goals.

- **Marketing:** acquire, promote, sell.
- **Learning:** discover, learn, complete, access certificates.

Mixing them created unnecessary competition for the user's attention.

**Key product decision:** separate the marketing experience from the learning platform, so each can serve its own goal without competing for the student's attention.

## What was the design principle?

Reduce cognitive load, and improve focus and retention. Every major decision was checked against three questions:

1. Can users understand where they are?
2. Can users quickly find what they need?
3. Can users continue learning without unnecessary friction?

The goal was not to add more features. It was to make the existing learning journey easier to understand and navigate.

## How did you work with engineering?

I worked with the technical team from the start to understand the limits of the WordPress implementation and to evaluate a more flexible custom platform. The goal was a platform that is easier to maintain, scale, and evolve, not a reskin of the old structure.

- **Design:** user research synthesis, information architecture and user flows, component system, interaction design, prototyping.
- **Engineering:** technology evaluation, architecture planning, platform feasibility, scalability assessment.

## How is the new platform organized?

The new platform is organized around the student's main tasks. Core navigation:

- **All Courses:** discover available courses.
- **My Courses:** continue courses already purchased or started.
- **My Certificates:** quickly access completed certifications.

Main learning journey: Discover, Course Detail, Learning, Completion, Certificate.

## What does the student journey look like?

Each step was designed to reduce uncertainty and cognitive load, so students can focus on learning and not on navigating the platform.

1. **All Courses:** a clear entry point for discovery.
2. **Course Detail:** what's inside, who it's for, how long.
3. **Start Course:** enrollment without friction.
4. **Course Player:** a focused, distraction-free learning environment.
5. **Progress:** visible progress reduces abandonment.
6. **Completion:** a clear end state.
7. **Certificate:** immediate access to proof of completion.

## What were the key UX decisions?

1. **Clear navigation.** Problem: the original navigation mixed marketing links with platform tools. Decision: a simplified structure that makes the student's main tasks visible, with no confusion between catalog and personal library.
2. **My Courses, continue learning.** Problem: students who had purchased courses had no clear place to resume them. Decision: a dedicated space for continuing learning, the most frequent student task, always visible from the main navigation.
3. **Course discovery.** Problem: the catalog and the personal library were mixed, so "courses I can buy" and "courses I own" were confused. Decision: All Courses is a clear entry point for discovering new courses, visually distinct from the student's library.
4. **Focused learning dashboard.** Problem: the dashboard was buried among marketing content and promotional banners. Decision: separating the platform from marketing lets the dashboard focus on the student's current progress and next steps.
5. **Certificate access.** Problem: students had no clear way to access, download, or share their certificates, a key value moment of the product. Decision: certificates are a primary navigation item, so students can reach their proof of completion right away.

## What about the design system?

The visual system supports a focused learning environment. It is meant to disappear into the content.

- **Typography:** Plus Jakarta Sans, a modern, readable typeface chosen for clear hierarchy.
- **Color:** a dark base with a single primary green (`#249480`), a subtle surface tone, and white text.
- **Icons:** Iconify, for a flexible and consistent icon system across the platform.

## What was the final product?

The new learning experience includes the dashboard, My Courses, All Courses, the course player, and certificates. The dashboard shows the student's current progress and next steps, without marketing noise.
